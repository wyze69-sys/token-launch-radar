How the radar values a launch

The multiple is learned, not picked

For every category, the radar takes the protocols in the snapshot whose token already trades, and uses the median of their market-cap-to-TVL ratios, shrunk toward the global median by the count behind it. A category with fewer than three traded tokens uses the global median instead: one protocol's ratio is an anecdote, not a market. A lending protocol is therefore valued the way the market values lending protocols, on this snapshot's own figures.

Momentum and maturity adjust it

A protocol whose TVL grew over the last seven days earns up to a 24% premium and one that shrank takes up to a 24% discount. A protocol tracked for years with no token earns up to a 10% maturity premium: it has had every chance to issue, so the launch it still owes is the more credible for the wait.

What the board leaves out

Protocols that already record a token, and wrapped, staked or bridged products of a token that trades, are not waiting on a launch of their own and never appear. An implied value is this radar's own claim about a launch that may never happen, not a prediction and not advice.
